## 1. How to compare deals (the rules we landed on)

1. **Normalise to £0 deposit.** A deposit is just a prepayment; "£359/month with £3,000 down"
   and "£430/month with £430 down" cost exactly the same (Richmond Kona PCP, *pasted*).
2. **Compare total cost over the term**, not the monthly. Dealers move money between deposit,
   mileage allowance and GFV to make the monthly look right.
3. **PCP vs PCH:** PCH total rentals + fees ÷ months, versus PCP deposit + monthlies + fees
   minus *realistic* equity ÷ months. Assume £0 equity in the base case.
4. **The GFV is a floor, not a forecast.** With liquidity, a low GFV just means repaying
   principal faster. Show the effective cost at several plausible resale values.
5. **APR is only meaningful against the best confirmed cash price for the same car.** The
   finance seller's own "cash price" is not the benchmark. Two separate numbers per deal:
   - *Acquisition-price penalty* = net vehicle price under the PCP (after contributions)
     minus best confirmed like-for-like cash price.
   - *Funding premium if bought at the end* = deposit + all instalments + balloon + fees
     minus best confirmed cash price. Annualise using the actual payment dates to get the
     **effective rate versus the cash alternative**. Example: Kia EV3 GT-Line S at a nominal
     3.9% works out at ~8.5% against a £37,263 broker cash price (*cited*).
6. **Finance contributions can make PCP cheaper than cash** (Inster and Ioniq 5: 0% plus
   £3,000 contribution). "Cheap cash or cheap finance, pick one" is not universal; ask whether
   the discounted price can be combined with the supported PCP. Drive the Deal says
   manufacturer schemes can normally be accessed through its supplying dealers (*cited*).
7. **For cash vs PCP ending in ownership, resale value is irrelevant**: same asset either way.
   For lease vs ownership it matters: show the break-even resale value.
8. **Used-car negotiation is mostly dead** (algorithmic pricing); new-car PCP is still
   negotiable by making dealers compete against a written quote (*cited*).

## 2. Market context (as of 6 Oct 2026)

- Q4 finance reset on 1 October. Hyundai Kona went from 0% / no contribution (orders to
  30 Sep) to 2.9% + £1,750 contribution (orders 1 Oct 2026 - 4 Jan 2027). Ioniq 3 went from
  5.9% at launch (Aug 2026) to 8.9% in October, just as it received the full £3,750 grant.
- Hyundai is lending at 0% on Inster, Ioniq 5 and Ioniq 9 at the same time it charges 8.9%
  on Ioniq 3. The 8.9% is a model-specific commercial decision ("launch-model pricing
  discipline"), not the rate environment.
- UK BEV share 26.2% YTD vs the 33% ZEV mandate target. September BEV registrations +36% YoY.
  Manufacturers still have a regulatory reason to subsidise EV registrations (*cited*).
- Oil shock: Brent ~$102, Saudi East-West pipeline hit in September, tanker capacity
  constrained, Chinese fuel exports suspended, UK diesel over £2/L. RAC: 30% of drivers more
  likely to consider an EV. Risk: rising EV demand removes the need for dealers to subsidise
  stock, so the freak deals disappear. Hence "shop in October".
- Hyundai Finance's Ioniq 5 GFV has collapsed from ~52-55% of transaction price (Tristan's
  2022 deal) to ~40% of the discounted price (£17,529 on £43,997). Even 0% cannot recreate
  the old £540 monthly: the new 84kWh Premium is £652 at £0 down.

## 5. Benchmarks worth remembering

- Richmond Kona 65kWh Advance PCH (expired 30 Sep 2026): £3,000 + 47 × £279 over 48 months =
  **£336/month effective**, 5k miles. The "strong deal" yardstick for a Kona-class car:
  £330-350 effective is lock-it territory; £400+ effective, ask why.
- Richmond Kona Advance PCP 0% (expired): £15,931 to hand back over 37 months = ~£431/month.
- Hyundai Ioniq 5 84kWh Advance PCH (Richmond, *cited*): £319 + £3,750 upfront, 48 months
  = ~£397/month effective.

| Carfile, Drive the Deal, New Car Discount, New UK Cars, Forces Cars Direct | Broker price listings | Need spec, model year and delivery confirmation |
| Carwow | Dealer offers with full PCP representative examples on model pages; some dealer-offer screens now hide the PCP structure ("illustrative only") | |

Nobody has built the combined thing: discover the best cash price, then rank every PCP and
lease against it. That is what this repo is for.

## 7. The enquiry that turns a listing into evidence

> Please confirm your all-in outright price for this exact specification, for December
> delivery, including all fees and any grant. Confirm model year, whether it is new and
> unregistered, and any eligibility or finance conditions. Separately, quote the same car on
> manufacturer PCP with £0 customer deposit, 36 monthly payments and 6,000 miles annually,
> showing the vehicle price, contributions, APR, balloon and fees. Can the discounted price
> be combined with the supported PCP?
