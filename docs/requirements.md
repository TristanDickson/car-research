## Hard requirements

1. Battery EV.
2. **Heat pump.**
3. **Internal 3-pin (230V) V2L socket in the cabin.** External V2L from the charge port is a
   plus, not a substitute. This is the requirement that most often forces a trim jump.
4. Fits a family of four with two child seats and a buggy. Four proper seats is the floor
   (Inster), five is normal, seven is a bonus.
5. Available for December 2026 handover (new) or buyable now (used).
6. If financed: £0 customer deposit.

## Strong preferences

- **Narrower and more manoeuvrable than the Ioniq 5** (1,890mm wide, big turning circle). A
  known annoyance, not a dealbreaker: the PV5 is 5mm wider and still on the list.
- **Range:** coming from a long-range Ioniq 5. ~300 miles WLTP is comfortable; ~240 is fine
  for family use but a real downgrade on long trips.
- **DC charging:** the Ioniq 5's 800V / 260kW system was genuinely useful. ~110kW (Ioniq 3,
  Kona) is "merely adequate".
- **Budget:** Patricia's ceiling is **£400/month effective**. Tristan is willing to go to
  ~£700/month for the PV5 because the utility is real and affordable. Resolve by pricing the
  PV5 against the best ≤£400 car that meets the hard requirements and asking whether the
  delta buys enough.
- **Liquidity is not a constraint.** Large savings; buying outright is a live option. The
  headline monthly is a cash-flow number, not the economic cost.
- **Finance posture:** PCP by default for optionality; PCH if it is £50-75+/month cheaper
  like-for-like; cash when the finance is punitive (Ioniq 3 at 8.9%). Finance it when the
  manufacturer is effectively paying you to (Inster, Ioniq 5 at 0% + £3k contribution).
